# How we pick the idea

Research is landing from two directions: (1) underserved gaps with first-person
evidence, (2) what wins accessibility hackathons and what gets mocked.

Every candidate gets scored 1-5 on six axes. We are explicitly NOT optimizing for
"most impressive demo" alone — the disability-dongle failure mode is a project that
demos beautifully and no disabled person would ever use.

| Axis | Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Real demand** | Have actual disabled users asked for this, in their own words? | The single best predictor of non-condescending design. Score 1 if we inferred the need ourselves. |
| **Underserved** | Is the status quo expensive, proprietary, abandoned, or absent? | Building a worse free copy of a great product helps nobody. |
| **Newly possible** | Does recent tech (on-device models, browser APIs) unlock this now? | Explains why it doesn't already exist — and makes a good demo narrative. |
| **Hackathon-tractable** | Can a convincing, honest version ship in the window? | Honest = no faked outputs, no wizard-of-oz demos. |
| **Demos in 3 min** | Can a judge *feel* the barrier, then feel it removed? | Transformation must be visible/audible live, not described on a slide. |
| **Survives Monday** | Could someone actually use this after the hackathon ends? | The projects that endure are the ones deployable without us. |

## Disqualifiers (automatic no, regardless of score)

- **Solves a problem disabled people don't have.** The classic dongle trap: solving the
  discomfort of *observers* rather than a barrier named by users.
- **Requires the disabled user to carry/wear/buy something extra** when the barrier
  could be removed at the source instead.
- **Only works if we're standing there.** If the demo needs our laptop, our lighting,
  our API key, or our narration, it isn't a product.
- **Fakes capability.** Hardcoded "AI" output, cherry-picked single input, or accuracy
  claims we haven't measured.
- **Fails its own accessibility bar.** Must be keyboard-navigable and screen-reader
  usable, or we have no standing.

## Tie-breaker

Prefer the idea where **the barrier is removed at the source** over one where the
disabled person has to adapt. Fixing the environment beats issuing equipment.
